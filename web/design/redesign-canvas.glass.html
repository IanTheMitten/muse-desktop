Muse Player, redesigned

A night instrument that stopped being green. Warm charcoal housing, one accent taken from the cover that is playing, search folded into the library, art galleries for browsing, and a downloader that fits in one tab. Every screen below is drawn with this library's real data and real cover art.

VARIANCE 6MOTION 4DENSITY 4 775 tracks · 91 albums with art · 14 artists system: muse-player

01 The housing

Same rows, same tiles, two housings. Left is what ships today: a saturated green-black that argues with any cover that is not green. Right is the proposal: warm charcoal at chroma 0.014, low enough to stay neutral under any accent, warm enough to never read as the default near-black every other player uses.

Measured. Every ink clears AA on every surface it can land on: --ink 17.55:1, --ink-muted 9.54:1, --ink-faint 6.36:1 against the chassis, and 5.07:1 at worst against the floating sheet. The elevation ladder widens from 0.145 to 0.157 in lightness across four surfaces, so panels separate without a shadow doing the work.

02 The accent is the record signature

One accent, page-wide, taken from the cover that is playing. The hue travels; lightness stays at 0.80 and chroma is re-solved per hue against the sRGB ceiling, so contrast is a constant and a blue accent never silently clips. Covers with no usable chroma fall back to a fixed amber. These are 38 of this library's real covers and the hues below are the real output of that algorithm. Click any one to drive every board on this page.

Derived chroma cleared the floor in enough pixels Fallback cover is monochrome, amber 72° is used instead

Why it holds. Across all 14 hues measured, the accent lands between 7.78:1 and 10.98:1 against the chassis, so it can carry text, not just fill. In light theme the same hue is re-solved at lightness 0.50 and clears 5.46:1 at worst. Fourteen of these thirty-eight covers fall back, which is the honest result: a fixed accent still has to be good, because more than a third of the library will use it.

03 Home one screen, no mode toggle

The map is a square, hard left, with the two things you read rather than aim at underneath it. Everything you might actually start is on the right: three strips, each scrolling on its own, so panning one never moves the map. The dot on the plate is the track playing now, in the place its own emotion vector puts it.

The map now runs on the app's own measurements, not Spotify's. It used to plot track_features.valence/energy from the 29 GB mirror, 754 tracks. It now plots embeddings.emotion_vec: 772 tracks, produced here by audeering/wav2vec2-large-robust-12-ft-emotion-msp-dim on the audio and j-hartmann/emotion-english-distilroberta-base on the lyrics. More coverage, no dependency on a mirror that can be missing, and the axis is arousal as the models measure it rather than Spotify's "energy".

04 Where the centre goes the part that had to change

The models do not output a centred plane. Raw, this library is 63.6% intense and has four warm tracks, so quadrants split at zero give one crowded corner and three empty ones. Both plates below fix that and both land on the same counts. They differ in what they do to the shape.

Take the left one. Both split at the median, so both read 217 bright, 217 low, 169 intense, 169 warm. The right one then places every track by its rank, which spreads the plate evenly and is easier to aim at, but it deletes the one thing the map is for: MoodMap.tsx argues the map "is the library, and the shape it makes is different for every collection." Rank normalising makes every collection the same shape. The cost of the median: a band is now relative to the library, so a track can change band when the library grows. Store the two medians and recompute them on the nightly retrain, the way feature_norm_bounds already stores its bounds, rather than solving them per request.

05 Library, phone search and browse are one place

The search tab is gone. Its field lives at the top of the library, filtering whichever segment is open, and the freed tab slot pays for Add. Artists are circles, albums are squares, so the segment you are in is legible before you read the label. 775 tracks load once and filter on the client, so switching segments and typing are both instant.

Vibe search stays reachable as a chip inside the same field rather than a fifth tab: the moment you want it is the moment name search has just failed you.

06 Library, desktop

The rail carries the same four destinations plus what you played last. The gallery is the page; there is no shelf, no editorial row, nothing selling anything.

07 Now playing, desktop lyrics stop blocking the player

Lyrics and the queue share one right panel, two tabs, the way Spotify's now-playing sidebar and Apple Music's lyrics sidebar both work. The record stops spinning: in its place is the level meter the design doc already specified, driven by the real output level. Full-screen lyrics survive as the expand button on the panel, not as the only way to read them.

Feasible today. web/src/player/gain.ts already builds MediaElementSource -> GainNode -> destination on first play. The meter is an AnalyserNode inserted into that existing graph, not a new audio path. Lyric lines are redacted bars here on purpose; the app renders the real LRCLIB text.

08 Now playing, phone

Same two panels, stacked instead of beside. Swipe or tap between Lyrics and Up Next; the art and the transport never move, so the thumb keeps its target.

09 The player rises from the bar live, click it

Both boards below are working. Click the control bar and the player lifts out of it; the chevron, or the dimmed area behind it, puts it back. This reverses the earlier call to keep now playing as a route: the bar is the handle, so leaving the screen to see the record was always the wrong shape.

The artwork is one element, not two. Nothing cross-fades a small cover into a large one: a single <img> is authored at its full size and parked on the bar by a transform, so opening simply removes that transform and the picture travels. Only transform and opacity animate, which is the rule the rest of the app already follows, and prefers-reduced-motion drops the travel while keeping the state change. Keep the routes. /now and /lyrics should still resolve, opening with the sheet already up, so a bookmark and the back button keep working. The route stops being the way in; it does not stop existing.

10 Glass and light the room takes the colour of the record

Three lights, hue sampled from the artwork, anchored apart so the glass has somewhere to blur between them. The first card has no field, for comparison. The last is a monochrome cover falling back to amber, which is what a third of this library does. Hover any pane on a desktop and the glint follows the pointer.

Every number here was solved, not dialled. Each light is oklch(0.62 0.16 H) at alpha 0.38; only the hue comes from the art, so the brightest field the three can make is known in advance. The panes are the chassis at 0.52 with blur(28px) saturate(170%), which leaves them 48% see-through and still measures --ink at 13.2:1 and --ink-faint at 4.79:1 over the worst field, against a 4.5 floor. The lights are anchored apart for that reason: stacked at one point they compose to a field no readable pane could sit on, and the 0.52 figure assumes one light plus the tail of a second, which is what separate anchors actually produce. One rule falls out of it: --ink-faint reads 3.0:1 on the bare field, so faint ink belongs on a pane, never floating on the light itself.

What this is not. Liquid Glass refracts what is behind it, bending light the way curved glass does. CSS cannot do that: backdrop-filter blurs and saturates a backdrop, it does not displace it. What is here is blur, saturation, a rim lit from above and a pointer-tracked glint, which reads as glass without being it. Calling it lensing would be a lie about the build. Where glass goes: the control bar, the tabs and rail, the rising sheet, the Lyrics and Up Next panel, the activity sheet. Grids, tracklists, the Add screens and the mood plate stay solid, because if every surface floats then none of them do. It costs something. A blurred backdrop is repainted whenever what is behind it moves, and the rising sheet is a full-screen pane. Apple shipped a slider for exactly this; a Settings switch that drops --glass-blur to 0 and raises the pane to opaque keeps the app usable on a phone that cannot afford it, and costs one token to build.

This edits PRODUCT.md. Principle 6 currently reads "motion is a readout, never atmosphere", and an ambient field is atmosphere by name. The defensible version, and the one worth writing down: light is a readout too. The field carries one fact, which record is playing, and it holds still whenever that fact does.

11 Add, the whole flow three screens, one tab

One field that takes an artist name or a pasted link. Artist, then releases, then tracks, then it is queued. No wizard, no settings, no destination picker.

Two things the live catalog forced. Searching this mirror for Olivia Dean returns 240 releases, of which 166 are compilations she merely appears on and 70 of the rest are singles: the grid has to default to albums with singles behind a segment, or it is unusable. And Post Malone's discography contains four separate rows for The Diamond Collection (Deluxe) and three for Hollywood's Bleeding, one of which this library already owns in full while its duplicates read as owning nothing. Editions collapse into one card, and the in-library count is merged across them, or the screen lies about what you have.

12 Add, when it goes wrong real errors from this library

Activity is a strip at the top of Add, not a destination. Tapping it opens the sheet: what is running and which step it is on, what is waiting, and what failed. The error text below is the exact string sitting in this library's rows today.

13 Light theme

The same housing under a window at 10am. Not an inversion: separation is carried by hairlines rather than luminance steps, and the accent is re-solved at lightness 0.50 so it can still carry text.

14 The control bar, before and after

Today the bar spends a whole row on a scrubber and draws its transport with emoji, which render differently on every platform. After: one row, drawn icons at the nav's stroke weight, a 2px progress line instead of a scrubber row, and the queue one tap away.

One reversal to flag. Thumbs-down leaves the phone bar, where ControlBar.tsx:53 deliberately put it. It stays on the desktop bar and in now-playing. The phone bar has room for four targets at 44px and the queue is the one that earns the fourth slot.
